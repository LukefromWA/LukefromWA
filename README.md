# Hey, I’m Lucas 👋

I’m a Mathematics and Biology graduate from Western Washington University and an incoming master’s student in Mathematics at Indiana State University.

I’ve wanted to be a biologist since I was nine. Mathematics found me later through an ecological modeling project, and now most of my work lives somewhere between biology, mathematics, and computation.

My interests include computational genomics, mathematical epidemiology, scientific machine learning, and stochastic dynamical systems. My long-term goal is to develop mathematically grounded, interpretable methods for studying biological systems, with assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations made explicit.

## Research & Projects

### 🧬 [Rare-Disease Evidence Pipeline](https://github.com/LukefromWA/rare-disease-evidence-pipeline)

Developed for the 2026 MVA Hackathon, this reproducible R/Python/Stan/Snakemake workflow connects genomic variant prioritization, phenotype similarity, biological networks, and externally trained Bayesian models with mechanism-led drug-repurposing research.

It includes coverage-aware evidence integration, ranking-sensitivity analysis, frozen-model inference, sequence-context readthrough analysis, and source-linked literature synthesis.

The integrated implementation is on the [`workflow` branch](https://github.com/LukefromWA/rare-disease-evidence-pipeline/tree/workflow).

### 🦠 [Bayesian Stochastic Epidemiology](https://github.com/LukefromWA/bayesian-stochastic-epi)

Bayesian state-space and stochastic epidemic models exploring disease progression, waning immunity, and time-varying genomic influences, with an emphasis on mechanistic inference and uncertainty quantification.

### 📈 [Epidemiological Time Series](https://github.com/LukefromWA/Epidemiological-TimeSeries)

Infectious-disease time-series analysis using forecasting, decomposition, multichannel singular spectrum analysis, and lagged climate–outbreak relationships.

### 🔬 Poisson–Hankel–Koopman Genomic Surveillance

Ongoing research exploring numerical linear algebra, spectral methods, and operator-theoretic approaches to viral genomic evolution.

## How I Work

I like understanding why something works before I use it.

That often means working through the mathematics, implementing a method from scratch, and comparing it with established libraries before applying it to a biological question.

I care about keeping evidence, model assumptions, and interpretation distinct. A useful result should show not just what a model says, but what supports it, where it is sensitive, and what remains unknown.

This GitHub is a record of that process: research projects, reusable workflows, and things I’m learning along the way.
